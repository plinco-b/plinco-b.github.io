⇄ Where iPhone and Android differ

Collected from the captures above. Same product and same copy; the platform supplies the chrome.

  • H00 The app icon and its name. iOS prints the whole name, Plinco Sleep; this Android launcher truncates it to Plinco Sl….
  • S01 Create your account. iPhone offers Continue with Apple and Continue with Google; Android offers Google only.
  • S11 Sign in failed. iPhone stacks "Forgot password" over "OK" in a glass sheet; Android puts OK and FORGOT PASSWORD side by side, in capitals, in a Material dialog.
  • C01 Android only: "Let app always run in background?". Android asks to be exempted from battery optimisation (and the checklist has a Background row); iPhone has neither.
  • C12 Lower on the page. The Background row (battery) exists only on Android.
  • P05 Continuous monitoring: the honest warning. The dialog buttons read NOT NOW / ENABLE on Android and Not now / Enable on iPhone.
  • P18 Go on call: "Let Plinco call you". Android adds "Do Not Disturb may silence the call. Turn it off, or add Plinco as an exception."
  • R01 A night: the score. On Android this modal starts right under the status bar, so the title sits tight against 9:41.
  • R05 Share: the report as a picture. The share sheets are each platform's own.
  • R06 Save: the system asks for photo access, once. Android 12 asks for access to photos and media; iPhone asks for add-only access to Photos (it cannot read the library). (Expected from the app's behaviour; the iPhone capture is still pending, so this is not yet confirmed on screen.)

Not capturable, or deliberately not captured

  • Tablets (iPad, Android tablets) are deliberately left out: this deck is mobile-first.
  • The first-run moments for a brand-new account (the confirm-your-email welcome, three puppies trotting up, the calibration "too quiet" warning on iPhone) already exist in Dave's own captures; they need a fresh account to reproduce and are not duplicated here.
  • The off-network sequence (wolf, chase, "Protected by a puppy") needs two phones on two different networks, or a developer build with the forcing lever; a production build cannot reach it.
  • Purchases are sandbox only (TestFlight is always sandbox; on Google Play only License testers buy free). The Android emulator's Play Store is a stub with no account, so "Restore purchases" there reports "The device or user is not allowed to make the purchase"; that is the emulator, not the app, and is not shown.
  • The Android "mic may not be working" screen (C09) comes from the emulator, whose microphone is silent. A capture from a real phone with a muted mic can replace C09_calibration_results_modal.png in the Android folder.
  • The chief's Banish and Overthrow buttons only appear for the village owner (or the chief phone), so they are not on these captures. Delete account, Leave this village and "Delete my account" are never confirmed in any capture: they would change the test village.
  • The in-app Terms still read "Placeholder copy. Full terms pending legal review." (lawyer review is open work).
